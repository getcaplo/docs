# Customer identity

> Verify who your customers are in the website widget, so tools can act on their own accounts and only verified customers see private content.

An agent on your website talks to anonymous visitors. Before a tool shows someone their orders or changes their address, Caplo must know who that person is, and the answer must not come from the model or the chat: a visitor can type any name or account number. **Customer identity** lets visitors prove who they are through a sign-in you control. Caplo records the result as a verified binding for the conversation, and tools receive the verified customer from Caplo, never from the model. Verification is available in the [website widget](/channels/website-widget/install/), in text and voice. WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram and phone conversations are always anonymous. ## What verification unlocks [Section titled “What verification unlocks”](#what-verification-unlocks) * **Customer-aware tools** act for the verified customer and can send your API a signed customer context token. See [Build customer-aware tools](/tools/customer-aware-tools/). * **Knowledge for verified customers only**: knowledge bases with the audience **Verified customers only** answer only after the visitor verifies. See [Knowledge](/agents/knowledge/). * **Previous conversation**: the channel can show a verified customer their most recent earlier conversation. ## Connection types [Section titled “Connection types”](#connection-types) You set up the ways customers can verify as **connections** in the console under **Settings** then **Customer identity**. Each connection says how a customer proves who they are and which ID in your system they become (the `sub`). Managing customer identity needs the Owner or Admin role. | Connection type | How the customer verifies | Use it when | | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | **Host sign-in** | Your website’s backend signs a short-lived assertion for the person already signed in on your site. The widget gets it from your page; the visitor does nothing. | Customers use the widget on pages where they are already signed in. This is the strongest and smoothest option. See [Host sign-in with signed assertions](/identity/signed-assertions/). | | **Provider sign-in** | The visitor signs in with your OpenID Connect provider in a popup window. | Your customers have accounts at an OpenID Connect provider, and the widget runs on pages where they are not signed in. See [Provider sign-in with OpenID Connect](/identity/oidc/). | | **Verification code** | The visitor enters their email, phone number or account number. Caplo asks your webhook to send a one-time code to the contact details you have on file. | You have no customer sign-in, or need a fallback. Codes always verify at the lowest level. See [Verification codes](/identity/verification-codes/). | Connections that share a **Subject namespace** identify the same customers, so a customer who verifies with a code and later with host sign-in is the same person to Caplo. The namespace defaults to the connection’s issuer (a verification code connection gets a namespace of its own) and cannot change after the connection exists, so set it when you create the connection if several connections should share customers. ## Assurance levels [Section titled “Assurance levels”](#assurance-levels) Each verification has a level that says how strongly the customer proved who they are. | Level | Typical source | | ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | `low` | A verification code. A sign-in your mapping rates low. | | `standard` | A normal password sign-in. Host and provider sign-ins without an `acr` claim get this level by default. | | `high` | A sign-in with multi-factor authentication. A `high` requirement also needs the customer to have authenticated within the last 15 minutes, unless you set a freshness. | For host and provider sign-in, the level comes from the `acr` claim through the connection’s **Assurance mapping**: you list each `acr` value your sign-in sends and the level it means, and choose what happens **When acr is absent** (**Low**, **Standard** or **Reject the sign-in**). An `acr` value you did not list is refused. Verification codes are always `low`. When an operation needs a higher level or a more recent sign-in than the customer has, the widget asks them to **Confirm it’s you**, and the operation waits until they do. This is called step-up. ## The verification policy [Section titled “The verification policy”](#the-verification-policy) The **Verification policy** panel on the same page decides whether visitors can verify at all and what a verification grants: ![The Verification policy card in Settings, Customer identity](/_astro/verification-policy.BkRtHs3s_Z2amgTI.webp) * **Verify customers in support conversations** must be on. * **Connections, in order**: up to 8 connections customers may use. A connection must be listed here and on the channel to be offered. * **Allowed scopes**: what a verification may grant. `identity:bind` is required. * **Minimum level per scope**: the level each scope needs. Without a row, `identity:bind`, `knowledge:verified` and tool reads need `low`; tool writes and memory need `standard`. * **Session timeouts**: how long a verification lasts at each level, idle and absolute. The defaults are 30 minutes idle and 4 hours absolute for `low`, and 60 minutes idle and 12 hours absolute for `standard` and `high`. | Scope | Grants | | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | | `identity:bind` | Verifying at all. | | `knowledge:verified` | Knowledge bases for verified customers only. | | `memory:read`, `memory:write` | Long-term customer memory, for agents that use it. | | `tools:read:<operation>`, `tools:write:<operation>` | One verified tool operation on a channel. | Caution Saving the policy ends every current verification: verified visitors must verify again. Change it outside busy hours. With **Require a second approver** on (under **Trust changes**), creating a connection, enabling it, or changing its issuer, keys, endpoints, webhook, mappings, attributes or API audiences waits as a pending trust change until another Owner or Admin approves it. Disabling a connection and revoking a key always apply at once. Trust changes are recorded in the [audit log](/operate/audit-log/); nobody is notified, so tell the approver. ## How verification works in the widget [Section titled “How verification works in the widget”](#how-verification-works-in-the-widget) Each website channel decides who may verify. In the console, open the channel, select **Configure & install**, and go to the **Conversation** tab. Under **Customer verification**, choose **Anonymous visitors only**, **Visitors may verify who they are** or **Visitors must verify before chatting**, then select **Choose sign-in methods and consent for this channel** to pick the connections and consent. See [Website widget settings](/channels/website-widget/settings/#customer-verification). 1. The widget offers the channel’s provider sign-in and verification code methods in order, as buttons labelled with the button label you set on the channel or, without one, the connection’s name. 2. With the consent mode **Ask for agreement before the first verification**, the visitor agrees to your consent text first. With **Show a notice after verification**, they verify straight away and then see a notice, such as “Signed in. This chat can see your account.”, with **Not you? Sign out**. 3. Host sign-in is never a button. It runs on its own when your page has registered a token provider and the consent mode is **Show a notice after verification**. 4. Once verified, the widget shows who is signed in, and tools, knowledge and history unlock as the policy allows. 5. Visitors can **Sign out** at any time. You can also revoke verifications from the console or from your own systems. See [Account links and revocation](/identity/account-links-and-revocation/). ## How the customer reaches your tools [Section titled “How the customer reaches your tools”](#how-the-customer-reaches-your-tools) A tool that declares `subject` in `caplo.yaml` receives the verified customer in `ctx.subject`: `externalSubject` is the `sub` your sign-in asserted, and `level` is the assurance level. For a stronger guarantee, Caplo can also give the tool a **customer context token**: a 60-second JWT, signed by your workspace’s issuer, that your own API verifies before acting. See [Build customer-aware tools](/tools/customer-aware-tools/) and [Verify customers in your API](/identity/verify-in-your-api/). Caution A customer-aware tool runs only when its operation is listed in the channel’s **Verified tool operations**, with a scope such as `tools:read:list_my_orders` that the policy allows. The console shows those operations and lets you change their level, freshness and token audience, but adding an operation to a channel is not yet available in the console or the CLI. Contact [support](/resources/support/) with the channel and the tools to set it up. ## Setup checklist [Section titled “Setup checklist”](#setup-checklist) 1. Choose a connection type and create the connection in **Settings**, **Customer identity**: [host sign-in](/identity/signed-assertions/), [provider sign-in](/identity/oidc/) or [verification codes](/identity/verification-codes/). 2. Set the **Assurance mapping** so each `acr` value your sign-in sends gives the right level. 3. In **Verification policy**, turn verification on, add the connection, allow `identity:bind` and the scopes you need, and save. 4. On the website channel, allow verification, choose the sign-in methods and consent mode, and select **Save entry point**. 5. For host sign-in, add the token endpoint to your backend and call `identity.configure` on your page. See the [JavaScript API](/channels/website-widget/javascript-api/#sign-visitors-in-from-your-page). 6. For tools that act on the customer’s account, follow [Build customer-aware tools](/tools/customer-aware-tools/), and verify customer context tokens in your API. 7. Decide how sign-outs and revoked accounts reach Caplo: [Account links and revocation](/identity/account-links-and-revocation/). 8. Test the whole flow on a test channel, and watch **Activity** on the **Customer identity** page for failed verifications and their reasons. [Host sign-in](/identity/signed-assertions/)Sign visitors in from your own backend with a short-lived assertion. [Provider sign-in](/identity/oidc/)Let visitors sign in with your OpenID Connect provider. [Verification codes](/identity/verification-codes/)Send one-time codes through your own webhook. [Verify in your API](/identity/verify-in-your-api/)Check Caplo's customer context token before acting.

# Account links and revocation

> End customer verifications from the console or from your own identity systems, handle sign-out in the widget, and rename or merge customer IDs.

A verification lasts until the visitor signs out, the session timeout for its level runs out, or something ends it early. This page covers the ways to end it early: from the console, from your identity provider or backend with back-channel logout and Shared Signals, and from the widget. It also covers renaming and merging customer IDs, and account links. Revoking a verification never deletes a conversation. The visitor loses verified access at their next turn and is asked to verify again. ## Revoke from the console [Section titled “Revoke from the console”](#revoke-from-the-console) On **Settings**, **Customer identity** (Owner or Admin): * **Revoke bindings**, on the **Activity** panel, opens a dialog where you choose what to revoke, the **Connection** and a **Reason**: * **Every binding of one customer**: enter the **Customer subject (sub)**. * **Every binding verified under a key**: enter the **Key ID (kid)**. * **Every binding of a connection**. * **Revoke subject**, next to a binding under **Recent bindings**, revokes that customer. * **Disable** on a connection stops new verifications through it and revokes every binding made through it. **Enable** brings it back, as a trust change; earlier visitors must verify again. * **Revoke key** on a host sign-in connection refuses that key from now on and revokes the bindings verified with it. See [Rotate keys](/identity/signed-assertions/#rotate-keys). Revocations take effect at once, even when **Require a second approver** is on, and are recorded in the [audit log](/operate/audit-log/). ## Back-channel logout [Section titled “Back-channel logout”](#back-channel-logout) When a customer signs out of your site or your provider, you can end their verification in Caplo too, with an OpenID Connect back-channel logout token. This works for host sign-in and provider sign-in connections. 1. On the connection, turn on **Back-channel logout** and save. Approve the trust change if your workspace requires a second approver. 2. Open the connection again with **Configure** and copy the **Back-channel logout URL**: `https://api.caplo.si/v1/identity/customer-signals/<CONNECTION_ID>/backchannel-logout`. 3. Register it as the client’s back-channel logout URL at your provider, or have your backend call it when a session ends. Caplo expects a form `POST` with one field, `logout_token`, holding a JWT that: | Part | Rule | | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | Header `typ` | `logout+jwt`. | | Signature | A key Caplo already trusts for the connection: your host sign-in keys (not revoked, with an allowed algorithm) or your provider’s JWKS. | | `iss` | The connection’s issuer, or the provider’s issuer. | | `aud` | For host sign-in, the connection’s audience (`urn:caplo:customer-identity:<CONNECTION_ID>`). For provider sign-in, the client ID. | | `iat`, `exp` | `iat` within the last 24 hours and not in the future; `exp` optional. | | `jti` | Required and unique. Each token works once. | | `events` | Contains `http://schemas.openid.net/event/backchannel-logout`. | | `sid`, `sub` | At least one. With `sid`, only verifications from that session end; it must match the `sid` of the assertion or ID token. With only `sub`, every verification of that customer through the connection ends. | | `nonce` | Must be absent. | Caplo answers `200` when the token is accepted, and `400` without detail when it is not. ## Shared Signals [Section titled “Shared Signals”](#shared-signals) If your identity provider or backend can transmit Shared Signals (OpenID SSF) events, Caplo can react to them as they happen. 1. On the connection, turn on **Shared Signals (SSF)** and save. 2. Open it again and copy the **SSF push URL** and the **SSF stream audience**. 3. Configure your transmitter to push (RFC 8935) to that URL, with that audience, signed with the same keys as above. Each Security Event Token is sent with `Content-Type: application/secevent+jwt`, has header `typ` `secevent+jwt`, `iss` the connection’s issuer, `aud` the stream audience, a unique `jti` and an `iat` within the last 24 hours, and up to 8 events. It must not have a top-level `sub`. Name the customer in `sub_id` or each event’s `subject`, in the format `iss_sub` (with `iss` the connection’s issuer and `sub` the customer’s ID) or `opaque` (with `id` the customer’s ID). | Event | What Caplo does | | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | CAEP `session-revoked` | Revokes the customer’s verifications through the connection. | | RISC `account-disabled` | Revokes the customer’s verifications through the connection. | | CAEP `credential-change` | Revokes verifications authenticated before the event’s `event_timestamp`. | | CAEP `assurance-level-change` | Lowers the level of current verifications to the level `current_level` maps to through the connection’s **Assurance mapping**. It never raises a level, and a value you did not map revokes. | | RISC `identifier-changed` | Proposes renaming the customer to `new-value`. The proposal waits under **Subject changes** until an Owner or Admin selects **Confirm**. | Other event types are accepted and ignored. Caplo answers `202` when the token is accepted, and `400` without detail when it is not. Every signal is recorded in the audit log. ## Sign-out in the widget [Section titled “Sign-out in the widget”](#sign-out-in-the-widget) Visitors can end their own verification: * **Sign out** in the widget, or **Not you? Sign out** on the notice shown after verifying. * **New conversation**, which signs them out first. When the visitor was signed in by your page with host sign-in, the widget does not sign them out on its own. It dispatches `caplo:identity-signout-requested` on the widget control, so your page can sign the user out of your site and then call `identity.signOut()`. Call `identity.signOut()` too whenever your user signs out on your site. See the [JavaScript API](/channels/website-widget/javascript-api/#sign-visitors-in-from-your-page). ## Rename or merge customers [Section titled “Rename or merge customers”](#rename-or-merge-customers) When a customer’s ID changes in your system, or two IDs turn out to be the same person, select **Rename or merge** under **Subject changes**: * **Rename: the customer’s ID changed** moves the customer from **Current subject** to **New subject**. * **Merge: two IDs are the same customer** folds the **Subject to merge away** into the **Subject to keep**. Choose the **Subject namespace** and give a **Reason**. Memory, account links and conversation history move with the customer, the old ID’s active sessions end, and the change is audited. Use this instead of sending a new `sub` from your sign-in: a new `sub` is a new customer to Caplo. ## Account links [Section titled “Account links”](#account-links) An account link maps a verified customer to an account in one of your backend systems, named by a namespace such as `billing`, and can hold a credential for that account. A verified tool operation that names the namespace then acts on the linked account, and Caplo passes the credential to the tool as one of its declared package secrets, only for that customer and only for that call. The value is never shown again after it is stored, and revoking a link destroys it. If a customer has no active link, the operation is refused, but they stay verified. Account links work together with verified tool operations on a channel. Neither can be set up from the console or the CLI yet; contact [support](/resources/support/) if you need them.

# Provider sign-in with OpenID Connect

> Let website visitors verify by signing in with your OpenID Connect provider in a popup window.

With **Provider sign-in**, the visitor signs in with your own OpenID Connect provider, such as the one your customer app uses. The widget opens the provider’s sign-in page in a separate window; Caplo runs the authorization code flow with PKCE, checks the ID token, and verifies the visitor as the provider’s `sub`. Use it when visitors are not already signed in on the pages that show the widget. If they are, [host sign-in](/identity/signed-assertions/) is smoother. ## What your provider needs [Section titled “What your provider needs”](#what-your-provider-needs) * The authorization code flow with PKCE (`S256`), returning the code in the query string. * The `iss` parameter in the authorization response (RFC 9207). Caplo refuses a callback without it. * ID tokens signed with `RS256`, `PS256` or `ES256`, with a `kid` in the header, from keys published at the provider’s JWKS URL, and valid for at most an hour. * A client registered for Caplo. Use a public client that needs no client secret. See [Client authentication](#client-authentication). Caplo calls your provider’s discovery, token and JWKS endpoints from the internet over HTTPS, without following redirects, so they must be publicly reachable. ## Set it up [Section titled “Set it up”](#set-it-up) 1. **Register a client at your provider** with: * Redirect URI: `https://api.caplo.si/v1/messaging/customer-login/callback`. The connection editor shows the same value as **Callback URL to register**; register it exactly. * Grant type: authorization code, with PKCE required. * Client type: public (no secret). * The scopes Caplo will ask for: `openid`, plus `profile` or `email` if you want the visitor’s name or email address. 2. **Create the connection.** In the console, go to **Settings**, **Customer identity**, select **Add connection**, and choose **Provider sign-in**: | Field | What to enter | | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | **Connection name** | Shown on the widget’s sign-in button unless the channel sets a button label. | | **Subject namespace** | Optional; defaults to the provider’s issuer. Fixed once created. | | **Provider configuration** | **Discovery URL**, such as `https://login.example.com/.well-known/openid-configuration`. Caplo reads the issuer and endpoints from it when you save. Or **Enter endpoints** for the issuer, authorization, token and JWKS URLs. | | **Client ID** | The client you registered. | | **Client authentication** | **None (public client with PKCE)**. | | **Requested scopes** | Space-separated, including `openid`. | | **Authorization details type** | Optional. When set, a step-up for a specific action sends an RFC 9396 `authorization_details` entry of this type with the action’s summary, for providers that show it. | | **Assurance mapping** | Each `acr` value your provider returns and its level, and the level **When acr is absent**. See [Assurance levels](/identity/#assurance-levels). | | **Accepted profile claims** | Which of `name`, `email`, `email_verified` and `locale` Caplo keeps from the ID token. Others are ignored. | Select **Create connection**. 3. **Add it to the policy.** In **Verification policy**, turn on **Verify customers in support conversations**, add the connection, make sure **Allowed scopes** includes `identity:bind`, and select **Save policy**. **Provider renewal period** sets how often Caplo renews a provider sign-in on its own, from 30 to 300 seconds. 4. **Turn it on for the channel.** On the website channel’s **Conversation** tab, allow verification under **Customer verification**, select **Choose sign-in methods and consent for this channel**, add the connection as a sign-in method with an optional button label, and select **Save entry point**. See [Website widget settings](/channels/website-widget/settings/#customer-verification). 5. **Test it** on a test channel: open the widget, sign in, and check **Activity** on the **Customer identity** page. Provider sign-in connections have no **Test assertion** action. ## Claims mapping [Section titled “Claims mapping”](#claims-mapping) | ID token claim | How Caplo uses it | | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | `sub` | Becomes the customer’s ID, the `externalSubject` and `sub` your tools and API receive. It must be stable. | | `acr` | Mapped to a level. An `acr` value that is not in the mapping refuses the sign-in. | | `auth_time` | When the customer authenticated. Required when Caplo asks for a recent sign-in (`max_age`). | | `sid` | Lets your provider’s [back-channel logout](/identity/account-links-and-revocation/#back-channel-logout) end this sign-in. | | `amr` | Kept with the verification, up to 16 values. | | `name`, `email`, `email_verified`, `locale` | Kept only when listed under **Accepted profile claims**. The widget shows `name` as “Signed in as”. | The ID token’s `aud` must be your client ID, and its `nonce` must match the one Caplo sent. The provider’s access and refresh tokens are not given to tools or agents. ## Step-up [Section titled “Step-up”](#step-up) When an operation needs a higher level or a more recent sign-in, Caplo starts a new sign-in with `acr_values` set to the mapped `acr` values that meet the level, and `max_age` set to the freshness, if any. Without a requirement, Caplo sends neither and no `prompt`, so a visitor already signed in at your provider is not asked again. Make sure your provider honors `acr_values` and `max_age` for the levels you map, and returns `acr` and `auth_time` in the ID token. ## What customers see [Section titled “What customers see”](#what-customers-see) 1. The widget shows a button with the connection name or the channel’s button label. 2. With the consent mode **Ask for agreement before the first verification**, the visitor reads your consent text and ticks **I agree to the terms above.** 3. Selecting the button opens your provider’s sign-in page in a separate window (“Sign-in opens in a separate secure window.”). If the browser blocks the window, the widget offers **Continue sign-in**. 4. After the visitor signs in, the window closes and the widget shows them as signed in, by name when the `name` claim is accepted. 5. For a step-up, the widget shows **Confirm it’s you**, and the same window opens again. ## Client authentication [Section titled “Client authentication”](#client-authentication) Use **None (public client with PKCE)**. **Client secret (basic)** needs Caplo to store the client secret for the connection, which you cannot do yourself yet: contact [support](/resources/support/) if your provider requires a confidential client. **Private key JWT** is not supported for provider sign-in yet.

# Host sign-in with signed assertions

> Sign visitors who are already signed in on your site in to the website widget, with a short-lived assertion your backend signs.

With **Host sign-in**, your website vouches for its own signed-in user. When the widget needs to know who the visitor is, it asks your page for a token; your page asks your backend, which checks its own session and signs a short **assertion** naming the customer. Caplo checks the signature against the public key you registered and verifies the visitor. The visitor clicks nothing, and Caplo never sees your passwords, cookies or private keys. In order: 1. The widget gets a one-time `nonce` from Caplo and calls your page’s `getToken` with it. 2. Your page posts the nonce to your backend. 3. Your backend checks its own session and returns an assertion for the signed-in customer, signed with your private key and valid for about a minute. 4. The widget sends the assertion to Caplo, which checks the signature, issuer, audience, nonce and times, and verifies the visitor as that customer. ## Before you start [Section titled “Before you start”](#before-you-start) * The Owner or Admin role, to manage **Customer identity**. * A website channel whose widget runs on pages where your customers are signed in. See [Install the website widget](/channels/website-widget/install/). * The [caplo CLI](/cli/install/), for generating keys and testing assertions. Nothing in `caplo identity` talks to your workspace. ## Set it up [Section titled “Set it up”](#set-it-up) 1. **Generate a signing key.** Run this where your backend’s secrets live: 
   ```plaintext
   caplo identity keygen --alg ES256 --kid 2026-11 --out ./caplo-keys
   ```
 It writes `2026-11.private.jwk.json` and `2026-11.private.pem` (readable only by you) and `2026-11.public.jwk.json`. The key ID defaults to the key’s thumbprint when you leave out `--kid`. Other algorithms are `ES384`, `PS256`, `RS256` and `EdDSA`. Store the private key in your backend’s secret store; it never goes to Caplo. 2. **Create the connection.** In the console, go to **Settings**, **Customer identity**, and select **Add connection**: | Field | What to enter | | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | **Connection type** | **Host sign-in**. | | **Connection name** | Up to 80 characters, shown in the console. | | **Subject namespace** | Optional. Leave it empty to use the issuer. Connections that share a namespace identify the same customers. Fixed once created. | | **Issuer (iss)** | The exact value your backend puts in `iss`, for example `https://www.example.com`. | | **Public keys** | **JWKS URL (recommended for rotation)**: an HTTPS URL that serves your public keys as a JWK Set. Or **Paste public JWKs (up to 4)**: the contents of `2026-11.public.jwk.json`. Every key needs `kid` and `alg`. | | **Allowed algorithms** | The algorithms your keys use. A key whose `alg` is not allowed is refused. | | **Assurance mapping** | Each `acr` value you send and its level, and the level **When acr is absent**. See [Assurance levels](/identity/#assurance-levels). | | **Claims and attributes** | The profile claims (`name`, `email`, `email_verified`, `locale`) and typed attributes you will send. Anything not listed is refused. | Select **Create connection**, then open it again with **Configure** and copy the **Audience (aud)**. It looks like `urn:caplo:customer-identity:<CONNECTION_ID>` and never changes. 3. **Add it to the policy.** In **Verification policy**, turn on **Verify customers in support conversations**, add the connection under **Connections, in order**, make sure **Allowed scopes** includes `identity:bind`, and select **Save policy**. 4. **Add a token endpoint to your backend.** See [Write the token endpoint](#write-the-token-endpoint). The connection editor also shows ready-made Node.js, Python and Go examples filled in with your issuer and audience. 5. **Register a token provider on your page** with the widget’s JavaScript API: 
   ```plaintext
   window.CaploWidget.get('<WIDGET_ID>').identity.configure({
     when: 'open',
     getToken: async ({ nonce, requirement }) => {
       const response = await fetch('/support/caplo-token', {
         method: 'POST',
         headers: { 'content-type': 'application/json', 'x-caplo-token-request': '1' },
         body: JSON.stringify({ nonce, requirement }),
       });
       return response.status === 200 ? response.text() : null;
     },
   });
   ```
 Return `null` when nobody is signed in on your page. `when` decides when the widget asks; call `identity.refresh()` after your user signs in or switches account and `identity.signOut()` after they sign out. See [Sign visitors in from your page](/channels/website-widget/javascript-api/#sign-visitors-in-from-your-page). 6. **Turn it on for the channel.** Open the website channel, select **Configure & install**, and go to the **Conversation** tab. Set **Customer verification** to **Visitors may verify who they are** or **Visitors must verify before chatting**, select **Choose sign-in methods and consent for this channel**, add the connection as a sign-in method, set **Consent mode** to **Show a notice after verification**, write the **Consent text** (16 to 4096 characters), and select **Save entry point**. Caution Host sign-in runs only with the consent mode **Show a notice after verification**. With **Ask for agreement before the first verification**, or without channel sign-in settings at all, the widget never asks your page for a token, and a channel that requires verification leaves the visitor unable to chat. ## Write the token endpoint [Section titled “Write the token endpoint”](#write-the-token-endpoint) The endpoint is yours, on your own domain. It must: * Accept only `POST` requests from your own site, and check the `Origin` header. * Require a custom header, such as `x-caplo-token-request: 1`, so a form on another site cannot call it. * Identify the visitor from your own session cookie (`SameSite`), never from the request body. * Take the issuer and audience from server configuration, never from the request. * Sign only the `nonce` and `requirement` it is given. * Answer `Cache-Control: no-store`, and `401` when nobody is signed in. 
```plaintext
// npm install jose
import { SignJWT, importPKCS8 } from "jose";


const ISSUER = "https://www.example.com";                       // the connection's Issuer (iss)
const AUDIENCE = "urn:caplo:customer-identity:<CONNECTION_ID>"; // the connection's Audience (aud)
const SITE_ORIGIN = "https://www.example.com";
const privateKey = await importPKCS8(process.env.CAPLO_SIGNING_KEY, "ES256"); // 2026-11.private.pem


// Express: app.post("/support/caplo-token", express.json(), caploToken)
export async function caploToken(req, res) {
  res.set("Cache-Control", "no-store");
  if (req.get("origin") !== SITE_ORIGIN || req.get("x-caplo-token-request") !== "1") return res.sendStatus(403);
  const user = req.session?.user;
  if (!user) return res.sendStatus(401);
  const { nonce, requirement } = req.body ?? {};
  if (typeof nonce !== "string") return res.sendStatus(400);
  if (requirement && !meets(user, requirement)) return res.sendStatus(401);
  const token = await new SignJWT({ nonce, acr: user.mfa ? "mfa" : "pwd", auth_time: user.authTime })
    .setProtectedHeader({ alg: "ES256", kid: "2026-11", typ: "caplo-customer+jwt" })
    .setIssuer(ISSUER)
    .setAudience(AUDIENCE)
    .setSubject(user.customerId) // stable and opaque, never an email or phone number
    .setIssuedAt()
    .setExpirationTime("60s")
    .sign(privateKey);
  res.type("text/plain").send(token);
}


// A step-up asks for a level, a recent sign-in, or both. Sign only when this session meets it.
function meets(user, { level, maxAuthAgeSeconds }) {
  if (level === "high" && !user.mfa) return false;
  if (maxAuthAgeSeconds !== undefined && Date.now() / 1000 - user.authTime > maxAuthAgeSeconds) return false;
  return true;
}
```

# Verification codes

> Let visitors verify with a one-time code that your own webhook sends to the email address or phone number you have on file.

With a **Verification code** connection, the visitor enters their email address, phone number or account number in the widget. Caplo generates a 6-digit code and asks your webhook whether that identifier belongs to one of your customers. If it does, your webhook sends the code to the contact details you already hold for that customer and tells Caplo their ID. The visitor types the code into the widget and is verified as that customer. Caplo never sends the code itself and never learns your customers’ contact details. Your webhook decides who matches and where the code goes. ## Set it up [Section titled “Set it up”](#set-it-up) 1. Build the webhook described below and deploy it at a public HTTPS URL. 2. In the console, go to **Settings**, **Customer identity**, select **Add connection**, and choose **Verification code**. Enter: * **Connection name**: shown on the widget’s button unless the channel sets a button label. * **Subject namespace**: if customers also verify through host or provider sign-in, enter the same namespace as that connection (by default, its issuer), so a customer is the same person whichever way they verify. Left empty, the connection gets a namespace of its own. You cannot change it later. * **Delivery webhook URL**: your webhook’s HTTPS URL. * **Accepted identifiers**: **Email**, **Phone** and **Account number**, as your webhook supports them. Select **Create connection**. 3. Copy the **JWKS URL** under **Customer context tokens** on the same page. Your webhook uses it to check Caplo’s signature. 4. In **Verification policy**, turn on **Verify customers in support conversations**, add the connection, make sure **Allowed scopes** includes `identity:bind`, and select **Save policy**. 5. On the website channel’s **Conversation** tab, allow verification, add the connection as a sign-in method, and select **Save entry point**. ## What visitors see [Section titled “What visitors see”](#what-visitors-see) 1. They select the button and see **Verify with a code**, then choose **Send the code using** (when you accept more than one identifier type) and enter the value. 2. After **Send code**, the widget always says “If that matches an account, we’ve sent a code. It expires in 10 minutes.”, whether or not the identifier matched, so nobody can use the widget to find out who your customers are. 3. They enter the **6-digit code** and select **Verify code**. **Use different details** starts again. Visitors must enter phone numbers in international format, such as `+15551234567`. Email addresses must be a plain address. Account numbers are 1 to 64 letters, digits, `.`, `_` or `-`, starting with a letter or digit. ## The webhook contract [Section titled “The webhook contract”](#the-webhook-contract) ### Request [Section titled “Request”](#request) Caplo sends one `POST` to the webhook URL for each code: 
```plaintext
POST /caplo/verification-codes HTTP/1.1
Host: hooks.example.com
Content-Type: application/json
Content-Digest: sha-256=:<BASE64_SHA256_OF_BODY>:
Signature-Input: caplo=("@method" "@target-uri" "content-digest" "content-type");created=1763200800;keyid="<KEY_ID>";alg="ecdsa-p256-sha256"
Signature: caplo=:<BASE64_SIGNATURE>:


{"challengeId":"5f0c2a9e-0b7e-4f7e-9a51-3f4d8f0c1d2e","identifier":{"type":"email","value":"ada@example.com"},"code":"482913","locale":"en-GB","expiresAt":"2026-11-15T10:10:00Z"}
```
 | Field | Meaning | | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | `challengeId` | The verification attempt. Each one gets one request; you can use it to ignore duplicates. | | `identifier.type` | `email`, `phone` or `account_number`. | | `identifier.value` | What the visitor entered, trimmed. Phone numbers are in E.164 form. | | `code` | The 6-digit code to send. | | `locale` | The visitor’s language, as a BCP 47 tag, for the message you send. | | `expiresAt` | When the code stops working, 10 minutes after it was made. | ### Signature [Section titled “Signature”](#signature) Every request is signed with your workspace’s key under HTTP Message Signatures (RFC 9421). The signature covers the method, the full webhook URL, `Content-Digest` (a SHA-256 of the body) and `Content-Type`. The algorithm is ECDSA P-256 with SHA-256, and `keyid` names a key in your workspace’s JWKS, the **JWKS URL** in **Settings**, **Customer identity**. Keys rotate every 90 days, so look the key up by `keyid` and fetch the JWKS again when you see a new one. Check, in order, before you look up anyone: 1. `Signature-Input` and `Signature` have the label `caplo` and exactly the components above. 2. `created` is within a few minutes of now. 3. `Content-Digest` matches the SHA-256 of the raw body you received. 4. The signature verifies over the signature base, built from your webhook URL as you entered it in Caplo (not the `Host` header your proxy passes on). ### Response [Section titled “Response”](#response) Answer `200` with JSON within 5 seconds: | Field | When | | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | | `matched` | Required. `true` when the identifier belongs to a customer and you sent the code; `false` otherwise. | | `sub` | Required when matched. The customer’s stable ID in your system, up to 255 characters. It becomes the verified customer’s `sub`, so use the same ID your other connections use. | | `delivery` | Optional. How you sent the code, such as `email` or `sms`: lowercase letters, digits and `_`. `sms` is recorded as an SMS one-time code. | 
```plaintext
{ "matched": true, "sub": "cus_1234", "delivery": "email" }
```
 ### Timeouts and retries [Section titled “Timeouts and retries”](#timeouts-and-retries) Caplo calls the webhook once, after it has already answered the visitor, and never retries. A timeout after 5 seconds, a status other than `200`, a body over 16 KiB, invalid JSON or a missing `sub` all count as a failed delivery: the code never verifies, and the visitor can ask for a new one. The URL must be HTTPS on a public address, and redirects are not followed. ### Example [Section titled “Example”](#example) 
```plaintext
// Express, Node.js 20 or later
import express from "express";
import { createHash } from "node:crypto";


const WEBHOOK_URL = "https://hooks.example.com/caplo/verification-codes"; // exactly as entered in Caplo
const JWKS_URL = process.env.CAPLO_JWKS_URL; // Settings, Customer identity, JWKS URL


let jwks = { fetchedAt: 0, keys: [] };
async function caploKey(kid) {
  if (!jwks.keys.some((k) => k.kid === kid) && Date.now() - jwks.fetchedAt > 30_000) {
    const response = await fetch(JWKS_URL, { redirect: "error" });
    if (!response.ok) throw new Error(`Caplo JWKS answered ${response.status}`);
    jwks = { fetchedAt: Date.now(), keys: (await response.json()).keys };
  }
  const jwk = jwks.keys.find((k) => k.kid === kid && k.kty === "EC" && k.crv === "P-256");
  if (!jwk) return null;
  return crypto.subtle.importKey("jwk", { kty: "EC", crv: "P-256", x: jwk.x, y: jwk.y }, { name: "ECDSA", namedCurve: "P-256" }, false, ["verify"]);
}


async function verifyCaploRequest(headers, rawBody) {
  const input = /^caplo=(\("@method" "@target-uri" "content-digest" "content-type"\);created=(\d+);keyid="([^"]+)";alg="ecdsa-p256-sha256")$/.exec(headers["signature-input"] ?? "");
  const signature = /^caplo=:([A-Za-z0-9+/]+={0,2}):$/.exec(headers["signature"] ?? "");
  if (!input || !signature || headers["content-type"] !== "application/json") return false;
  const [, params, created, kid] = input;
  if (Math.abs(Date.now() / 1000 - Number(created)) > 300) return false;
  const digest = `sha-256=:${createHash("sha256").update(rawBody).digest("base64")}:`;
  if (headers["content-digest"] !== digest) return false;
  const key = await caploKey(kid);
  if (!key) return false;
  const base = [
    `"@method": POST`,
    `"@target-uri": ${WEBHOOK_URL}`,
    `"content-digest": ${digest}`,
    `"content-type": application/json`,
    `"@signature-params": ${params}`,
  ].join("\n");
  return crypto.subtle.verify({ name: "ECDSA", hash: "SHA-256" }, key, Buffer.from(signature[1], "base64"), new TextEncoder().encode(base));
}


const app = express();
app.post("/caplo/verification-codes", express.raw({ type: "application/json", limit: "16kb" }), async (req, res) => {
  if (!(await verifyCaploRequest(req.headers, req.body))) return res.sendStatus(401);
  const { identifier, code, locale, expiresAt } = JSON.parse(req.body);
  const customer = await findCustomer(identifier.type, identifier.value); // your lookup
  if (!customer) return res.json({ matched: false });
  const delivery = identifier.type === "phone" ? "sms" : "email";
  await sendCode(customer, delivery, code, locale, expiresAt); // to the contact details on file
  res.json({ matched: true, sub: customer.id, delivery });
});
```
 ## Security rules [Section titled “Security rules”](#security-rules) * Send the code only to contact details you already hold for the customer. For an account number, use the email address or phone number on that account, never one the visitor typed. * Never put the code in the response, a log or an analytics event, and never send it anywhere except the customer. * Treat a request with a bad signature, digest or timestamp as hostile: answer `401` and send nothing. * Answer `matched: false` the same way for “no such customer” and “customer has no usable contact details”. * Rate-limit your own sending too. Caplo already limits codes to 3 per identifier and 10 per visitor address every 15 minutes, 60 per widget per hour, and 1000 per workspace per day. Each code works once, for 10 minutes, and allows 5 attempts; wrong codes count whether or not the identifier matched. Caplo stores codes and identifiers only as keyed hashes and deletes them after 24 hours. # Verify customers in your API

> Check Caplo's customer context token in your own API with verifyCustomerContext, refuse replays, and ask the customer for a stronger sign-in.

When a [customer-aware tool](/tools/customer-aware-tools/) calls your API, the API should not take the customer’s ID on trust from the tool. A **customer context token** lets the API check for itself that Caplo verified this customer, for this API, moments ago. The token is a JWT signed by your workspace’s own issuer. It lasts 60 seconds and is issued for one call. ## Before you start [Section titled “Before you start”](#before-you-start) 1. On the connection your customers verify through (**Settings**, **Customer identity**, **Configure**), list your API under **API audiences**: one HTTPS URL per line, up to 8. Tokens are only issued for these audiences. 2. Copy the **Issuer** and **JWKS URL** from **Customer context tokens** on the **Customer identity** page. The issuer looks like `https://api.caplo.si/v1/identity/workspaces/<WORKSPACE_ID>`. 3. On the website channel, under **Verified tool operations**, choose your API as the operation’s **customer token audience**. Adding operations to a channel is not self-serve yet; see [Customer identity](/identity/#how-the-customer-reaches-your-tools). 4. In the tool, send `ctx.subject.customerToken` as `Authorization: Bearer`. See [Build customer-aware tools](/tools/customer-aware-tools/#customer-context-tokens). ## Verify with `@caplo/tools/verify` [Section titled “Verify with @caplo/tools/verify”](#verify-with-caplotoolsverify) `verifyCustomerContext` is part of the `@caplo/tools` package on npm. It has no runtime dependencies: it uses only `fetch` and WebCrypto. The package targets Node.js 24. 
```plaintext
npm install @caplo/tools
```
 
```plaintext
import { CustomerContextError, verifyCustomerContext } from "@caplo/tools/verify";


const ISSUER = process.env.CAPLO_ISSUER!;      // Issuer from Settings, Customer identity
const AUDIENCE = "https://api.shop.example";   // this API, as listed under API audiences


export async function verifiedCustomer(request: Request) {
  const token = /^Bearer (\S+)$/.exec(request.headers.get("authorization") ?? "")?.[1];
  if (!token) return null;
  try {
    return await verifyCustomerContext(token, {
      issuer: ISSUER,
      audience: AUDIENCE,
      replayStore,                             // see below
      requiredScope: "tools:write:change_address",
    });
  } catch (error) {
    if (error instanceof CustomerContextError) return null; // answer 401
    throw error;
  }
}
```
 Then act only for `claims.sub`, the customer’s ID in your system. Never read the customer from the request body or query: that is what a model or a tampered request could change. ### Options [Section titled “Options”](#options) | Option | Required | Notes | | ----------------------- | -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | `issuer` | Yes | The exact issuer from the console. Keys are fetched from `<issuer>/.well-known/jwks.json`. | | `audience` | Yes | This API’s audience, exactly as listed on the connection. | | `replayStore` | No | Refuses a token whose `jti` was already used. Use one for every API that changes data. | | `requiredScope` | No | A scope the token must carry: the operation’s scope, such as `tools:read:list_orders` or `tools:write:change_address`. | | `clockToleranceSeconds` | No | Allowed clock skew, default 0. | Missing `issuer` or `audience` throws a plain `Error`, since that is a configuration mistake, not a bad token. ### What it checks [Section titled “What it checks”](#what-it-checks) A token is accepted only when all of these hold: * It is a compact JWS of at most 8 KiB, with header `typ` `at+jwt`, `alg` `ES256`, a `kid` and no `crit`. * `iss` equals your issuer, and the signature verifies with the key named by `kid` from the issuer’s key set. * `aud` equals your audience. * The required claims are present and well formed. * It lives at most 60 seconds (`exp` minus `iat`), has not expired, and was not issued in the future. * With `requiredScope`, `scope` contains it. With `replayStore`, its `jti` has not been used. The key set is cached for its `Cache-Control` max-age (5 minutes), and an unknown `kid` triggers a refetch at most every 30 seconds, so Caplo’s key rotation needs nothing from you. ### Errors [Section titled “Errors”](#errors) Every refusal throws `CustomerContextError` with a `reason`: | `reason` | Meaning | Answer | | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | | `malformed`, `typ`, `alg`, `claims` | Not a Caplo customer context token. | `401` | | `issuer`, `audience` | Issued by another workspace or for another API. | `401` | | `kid_unknown`, `signature` | Not signed by your workspace’s current keys. | `401` | | `expired`, `not_yet_valid`, `lifetime` | Outside its 60-second window. Check your server clock if this is frequent. | `401` | | `scope` | The token is for a different operation. | `403` | | `replayed` | The token was already used. | `401` | | `jwks_unavailable` | The key set could not be fetched. | `503`, so the call can be retried | ## Claims [Section titled “Claims”](#claims) | Claim | Meaning | | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | `sub` | The customer’s ID in your system, as your sign-in asserted it. | | `acr` | The verification level: `low`, `standard` or `high`. | | `auth_time` | When the customer last authenticated, in seconds, when known. | | `scope` | The operation’s scope, such as `tools:write:change_address`. | | `act` | The acting agent, `{ "sub": "caplo:agent:<AGENT_ID>" }`. When a person acts for another customer, `act.act` names that person and their `relationship`, and `sub` is the customer they act for. | | `attributes` | Attributes from the sign-in that the connection makes visible to **Tools**. | | `caplo.namespace` | The subject namespace the customer verified in. | | `caplo.conversation` | The conversation the call belongs to. | | `caplo.action`, `caplo.actionDigest` | For a write, the action the customer confirmed and a digest of it. Record them with the change. | | `client_id` | `caplo:workspace:<WORKSPACE_ID>`. | | `iss`, `aud`, `jti`, `iat`, `exp` | Standard JWT claims. | ## Refuse replays across instances [Section titled “Refuse replays across instances”](#refuse-replays-across-instances) `MemoryReplayStore` remembers token IDs in one process. When your API runs on more than one instance, give it a shared store: an object whose `use(jti, expiresAt)` returns `true` the first time it sees an ID and `false` after that. With Redis: 
```plaintext
import { createClient } from "redis";
import type { ReplayStore } from "@caplo/tools/verify";


const redis = createClient({ url: process.env.REDIS_URL });
await redis.connect();


export const replayStore: ReplayStore = {
  async use(jti, expiresAt) {
    const result = await redis.set(`caplo:jti:${jti}`, "1", { NX: true, PXAT: expiresAt.getTime() });
    return result === "OK";
  },
};
```
 ## Verify without the SDK [Section titled “Verify without the SDK”](#verify-without-the-sdk) Any JWT library works. Accept the token only when the header has `typ` `at+jwt` and `alg` `ES256`; verify it with the key from `<issuer>/.well-known/jwks.json` whose `kid` matches; require `iss` and `aud` to equal your values exactly, `exp` in the future and `exp - iat` at most 60; and record each `jti` until its `exp`. ## Ask for a stronger sign-in [Section titled “Ask for a stronger sign-in”](#ask-for-a-stronger-sign-in) When an operation needs a higher level or a more recent sign-in than the token shows, refuse it with an RFC 9470 challenge, using Caplo’s level names in `acr_values` and seconds in `max_age`: 
```plaintext
const fresh = claims.auth_time !== undefined && Date.now() / 1000 - claims.auth_time <= 300;
if (claims.acr !== "high" || !fresh) {
  return new Response(null, {
    status: 401,
    headers: { "WWW-Authenticate": 'Bearer error="insufficient_user_authentication", acr_values="high", max_age="300"' },
  });
}
```
 In the tool, turn the challenge into a step-up with `stepUpFromChallenge`, or ask for one directly with `stepUpRequired`: 
```plaintext
import { stepUpFromChallenge, stepUpRequired } from "@caplo/tools";


const stepUp = stepUpFromChallenge(response.headers.get("www-authenticate"));
if (stepUp) return stepUp;


// or, without a challenge from the API:
return stepUpRequired({ level: "high", maxAuthAgeSeconds: 300 });
```
 `stepUpFromChallenge` returns `undefined` for any other header. When `acr_values` lists several levels, it requires the weakest one listed; `max_age` above 86400 is capped. Caplo then asks the customer to **Confirm it’s you**. A write runs again with the same arguments once they have; for a read, the customer asks again. # Build customer-aware tools

> Let a tool act for the verified customer in the conversation, and verify Caplo's customer context token in your own API.

Some tools must act for one specific customer: “show my orders”, “change my delivery address”. The customer’s identity must not come from the model, because a model can be talked into passing someone else’s account number. A customer-aware tool instead receives the **verified customer** from Caplo, which knows who the visitor is because they signed in through your [customer identity](/channels/website-widget/settings/) setup. ## Before you start [Section titled “Before you start”](#before-you-start) * A customer identity connection in **Settings**, **Customer identity**, so visitors can verify who they are (for example with your own sign-in). * A channel whose customer verification lets or requires visitors to verify. See [Website widget settings](/channels/website-widget/settings/). ## Declare the subject [Section titled “Declare the subject”](#declare-the-subject) Add `subject` to the tool’s policy in `caplo.yaml`: 
```plaintext
tools:
  - name: list_my_orders
    effect: read
    timeoutSeconds: 10
    subject:
      policy: verified_required
      selector: account
```
 * `policy: verified_required` means the tool runs only for a verified customer. Caplo passes that customer in the call context, never through arguments. Tools without `subject` never receive it. * `selector` is optional. It names a string input property that Caplo fills with the customer’s resource reference on every call. The model cannot set it: a call where the model supplied it is refused. The property must be optional in the input schema (not listed in `required`), and it can never be shown in a [presentation](/tools/present-results/). For a function package, put `subject` at the top level beside `effect`. ## Read the customer in code [Section titled “Read the customer in code”](#read-the-customer-in-code) `ctx.subject` holds the verified customer. Call `ctx.requireSubject()` to get it or fail: it throws when the call has none, so the tool can never run unscoped. 
```plaintext
import { defineTool } from "@caplo/tools";


export default defineTool({
  name: "list_my_orders",
  description: "List the signed-in customer's recent orders.",
  inputSchema: { type: "object", properties: {}, additionalProperties: false },
  async handler(_args, ctx) {
    const customer = ctx.requireSubject();
    const response = await fetch(`https://api.shop.example/customers/${encodeURIComponent(customer.externalSubject)}/orders`, {
      headers: { authorization: `Bearer ${ctx.secrets.get("SHOP_API_KEY")}` },
      signal: ctx.signal,
    });
    if (!response.ok) throw new Error(`order list failed with ${response.status}`);
    return { orders: (await response.json()).orders.slice(0, 10) };
  },
});
```
 | Property | What it is | | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | | `externalSubject` | The customer’s ID in your system, as your sign-in asserted it (its `sub`). | | `subjectNamespace`, `subjectNamespaceRef` | The identity namespace the customer verified in. | | `resourceRef` | `subjectNamespaceRef:externalSubject`, a stable key to map to your customer account. | | `level` | How strongly the customer verified: `low`, `standard` or `high`. | | `subjectId`, `bindingId` | Caplo’s IDs for the customer and their sign-in. | | `customerToken` | A customer context token, when the channel is set to send one. See below. | Function packages announce support for the subject automatically. An MCP package must add the capability when it creates the server, or Caplo refuses to send it subject calls: 
```plaintext
import { SUBJECT_CONTEXT_CAPABILITY } from "@caplo/tools";


const server = new McpServer(
  { name: "accounts", version: "1.0.0" },
  { capabilities: { logging: {}, experimental: { ...SUBJECT_CONTEXT_CAPABILITY } } },
);
```
 ## Customer context tokens [Section titled “Customer context tokens”](#customer-context-tokens) Passing the customer ID to your API with a shared API key means your API trusts the tool completely. A **customer context token** lets your API check for itself that Caplo verified this customer for this call. It is a signed JWT that: * lasts 60 seconds and is meant for a single call; * names your API as its audience and your workspace’s Caplo issuer; * carries the customer’s ID in `sub`, the agent in `act`, the verification level in `acr`, and for a write, the confirmed action in `caplo.action` and `caplo.actionDigest`. ### Set it up [Section titled “Set it up”](#set-it-up) 1. In **Settings**, **Customer identity**, edit the connection your customers use and list your API under **API audiences**: one HTTPS URL per line, up to 8. Tools can only get tokens for these audiences. 2. On the same page, under **Customer context tokens**, copy the **Issuer** (and the **JWKS URL**, if your API needs it). Give them to whoever runs the API. ![The Customer context tokens card with the Issuer and JWKS URL](/_astro/customer-context-tokens.D1gznfiw_Z62Tlq.webp) 3. In the website channel’s settings, under **Customer verification**, find the tool’s operation in **Verified tool operations** and choose your API in its **customer token audience**. Adding an operation to a channel is not yet available in the console or the CLI: [contact support](/resources/support/) with the channel and the tools, then set the audience here. See [Customer identity](/identity/). 4. In the tool, send the token as a bearer token. It is not an enumerable property, so spreading or logging the subject does not leak it, but never log it yourself: 
   ```plaintext
   const customer = ctx.requireSubject();
   if (!customer.customerToken) throw new Error("no customer context token is configured for this tool");
   const response = await fetch("https://api.shop.example/me/orders", {
     headers: { authorization: `Bearer ${customer.customerToken}` },
     signal: ctx.signal,
   });
   ```
 ### Verify the token in your API [Section titled “Verify the token in your API”](#verify-the-token-in-your-api) Install `@caplo/tools` in your API and use `verifyCustomerContext` from `@caplo/tools/verify`. It has no runtime dependencies; it uses `fetch` and WebCrypto. 
```plaintext
import { CustomerContextError, MemoryReplayStore, verifyCustomerContext } from "@caplo/tools/verify";


const replayStore = new MemoryReplayStore();


export async function customerFrom(request: Request) {
  const token = request.headers.get("authorization")?.replace(/^Bearer /, "") ?? "";
  try {
    const claims = await verifyCustomerContext(token, {
      issuer: process.env.CAPLO_ISSUER!,          // the Issuer from Settings, Customer identity
      audience: "https://api.shop.example",       // one of the connection's API audiences
      replayStore,                                // refuse a token used twice
    });
    return { customerId: claims.sub, level: claims.acr };
  } catch (error) {
    if (error instanceof CustomerContextError) return null; // answer 401
    throw error;
  }
}
```
 `verifyCustomerContext(token, options)` refuses a token unless its type is `at+jwt`, it is signed with ES256 by a key from `<issuer>/.well-known/jwks.json`, its issuer and audience match exactly, it is valid now and lives at most 60 seconds, and, with a replay store, its `jti` has not been used. It returns the claims or throws `CustomerContextError` with a `reason` such as `issuer`, `audience`, `expired`, `signature` or `replayed`. | Option | Notes | | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | | `issuer` | Required. The exact issuer from the console. | | `audience` | Required. This API’s audience, as listed on the connection. | | `replayStore` | Recommended for write APIs. `MemoryReplayStore` suits a single instance; with several instances, implement `use(jti, expiresAt)` on a shared store, returning `false` for a used ID. | | `requiredScope` | A scope the token must carry. | | `clockToleranceSeconds` | Allowed clock skew, default 0. | Key sets are cached for their `Cache-Control` max-age, and an unknown key ID triggers a refetch at most every 30 seconds, so key rotation needs no change on your side. Caution Look the customer up by `claims.sub`, never by an ID in the request body. That is the point of the token: a model or a tampered request cannot change whose data your API returns. ### Ask for a stronger sign-in [Section titled “Ask for a stronger sign-in”](#ask-for-a-stronger-sign-in) When an operation needs a higher level or a more recent sign-in than the token shows, your API can answer `401` with an RFC 9470 challenge, for example `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer error="insufficient_user_authentication", acr_values="high", max_age="300"`. The tool passes it on with `stepUpFromChallenge`, and Caplo asks the customer to verify again. See [Ask the customer to verify again](/tools/write-tools/#ask-the-customer-to-verify-again).
